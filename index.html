What it does

Talk to it the way you’d talk to a very good assistant.

One name, then the request. Javis works out the rest.

Hears

Hands-free, on-device

Say its name and speak. Speech recognition runs on your Mac, replies come back in about a second, and you can talk over it to interrupt.

Sees

Looks at your screen

Ask about “this” and it looks: the error in front of you, the page you’re reading, the message you want answered.

Acts

Works in any app or browser

Clicks, types, fills forms, opens things. In Brave, Chrome, Safari, Mail, your editor. It checks the result before it says it’s done.

Remembers

Knows you across sessions

People, projects, preferences, how you like things done. Tell it once. It even learns your words when you correct a mishearing.

Speaks up

Reminders and routines

“In twenty minutes, remind me.” “Every morning at nine, my calendar and what’s waiting on me.” It runs them and tells you.

Grows

Improves itself

Ask for a new behaviour and Javis offers options on screen, edits its own code, runs its tests, and restarts. If a change breaks it, it rolls back and says so.

How it works

Ears on the Mac. A Claude brain. Hands in every app.

You speak

A floating orb sits over whatever you’re doing. Apple’s on-device recogniser hears you; nothing you say leaves the machine to be transcribed.

Javis thinks

Claude plans the work, asks you on a card when it needs a decision, and hands long jobs to background workers so it never goes quiet on you.

Javis acts, then looks again

Through the Mac’s own accessibility layer and the browser’s developer protocol, it clicks and types like you would, and verifies the result before reporting back in its own voice.

Why Javis

Every assistant so far has been a box you type into. Javis is someone you talk to, who can reach the keyboard.

  • Runs on your Mac. Local speech recognition and a local neural voice. Your screen is only looked at when you ask.
  • Does, then proves. It never says “done” without looking at the result.
  • Asks before anything irreversible. Sending, paying, deleting, posting: a card appears, and the last tap is yours.
  • Keeps a log. Everything it heard, did and got wrong is on record. Ask “what went wrong?” and it tells you.
  • Built for daily work. Mail, calendar, browsing, research, code chores. The repetitive hours, handed off.
